## Question 2

*Definition*

Phishing is the act of tricking the victim into providing their important, personal information willingly. This is usually achieved by impersonating as a legitimate individual, organisation, or a company. 

Breach of data, under the PDPA, is an event whereby unauthorised steal users' personal information. While both involve the act of stealing while the victim is unaware, phishing requires of the victim to act willingly, while data breaches do not; the hackers exploit the system's vulnerability.

*Comparisons*

1. Method
	- Phishing mainly focuses on social engineering 
	- Breach of data focuses on exploiting system's vulnerability
2. Law
	- Phishing is a cybercrime under the Cyber Crime Act
	- Data breaches are an issue of compliance under the PDPA act
3. Victim's role
	- Phishing requires the victims to provide information with their own volition
	- Data breaches can happen without the victims knowing
4. Who is punished
	- The company is not punished under the PDPA act if a phishing case took place involving the company's name
	- The company is punished if data breaches

*Example*
1. Carousel scam that took place where a young girl was told to install an app to transfer money
2. Public bank data breaches

## Question 5

1. Cyber squatting
	- This refers to when an individual who does not own the trademarked name of a company, registers the trademarked name as his domain name. This means that the trademarked domain name does not belong to the company but rather himself. This is usually done to force the company to pay the person.
2. Typo squatting
	- This refers to when a persons registers a domain that is intentionally spelled wrong.
	- This is usually done with the intention of luring or phishing. If the victim does not check the domain name properly, he will be tricked into providing his personal information willingly. 
	- This can also be done to force the creator of the correctly spelled site to pay money to take ownership of the incorrectly spelled site.
3. Gripe sites
	- Refers to sites with inappropriate domain names
	- For example, the site name could be making fun of someone else.
