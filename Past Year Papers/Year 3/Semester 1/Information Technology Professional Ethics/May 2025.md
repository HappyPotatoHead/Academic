## Question 1
### Question A

1. Deepfakes
	- The use of deepfakes itself is not illegal; however, it can violate several laws such as Section 233 of Communications and Multimedia Act when deepfake is used to represent a victim in a indecent manner and spreading said adulteration. For example, the use of deepfakes to create revenge porn which can cause irrevocable damage to the victim's reputation. 
	- Misuse of deepfake is also unethical, violating the principle of integrity in which the victim's reputation and image is tarnished for the perpetrator's own personal amusement or gain. For example, using deepfake to paste someone's face on an advertisement promoting illegal online gambling
2. Intellectual Property 
	- Using, distributing, reproducing, or claiming someone else's intellectual property as your own is illegal and is punishable under Copyright Laws. 
	- Ethically, such actions are dishonest and violates integrity, as it is essentially stealing someone else work and claiming to be original. 

### Question B

1. Strive for perfection
	- Any professional within the field of technology would constantly work to improve their skills.
	- For example, programmers can participate in workshops related to the use of AI to upskill themselves
2. Integrity
	- Professionals should be honest with their work and hold accountability
	- For example, a professional should not pirate software; instead purchase the license for the software
3. Diligent
	- Professionals should perform their tasks within a timely manner and thoroughly
	- For example, professionals should not complete a customer's request to build a website haphazardly, but instead fulfil customer's expectation and meet their satisfaction
4. Benevolent
	- Professionals should use their technological skills, facilities, and devices in a way that will provide benefit to the society
	- For example, whenever possible, professionals should make their creations accessible to those with disabilities. Or, professionals should take up projects that can improve the lives of others.
5. Professionalism
	- Professionals should treat each other or other people with respect and fairly
	- For example, managers should not prioritise one employee over the other simply because of the person's sex, age, or background
6. Competence
	- A professional should not falsify their qualifications or certifications to represent their capability.
	- For example, a professional should not fabricate their degree or any certifications such as CISCO's network certification to trick others into believing he or she has the related skills
	- A professional should be able to provide services within the area of competence claimed.

	- For example, attending workshops or tutorials to learn new programming languages.

### Question A

#### Question I

Malware is an umbrella term that describes the type of software capable of damaging the computer or computer materials that a user has, whereas virus is a specific form of malware. 

#### Question II

Spoofing is a type of attack whereby the hacker pretends to be someone or something, often to deceive computers, while phishing is a type of attack of impersonating a legitimate source to deceive individuals to provide personal information

#### Question III

Ransomware is the act of locking important files or threating to delete them unless the victims agree to pay an amount of money, while carding refers to using a stolen credit card information to make purchases

#### Question IV

Black hat hackers are hackers who hack illegally and with malicious intentions while white hat hackers are legal and ethical hackers employed to test and improve cybersecurity.

### Question C

1. Patent
	- This is used to provide the inventor of a novel, useful, and not obvious with exclusive rights to the invention.
	- Patents usually last 20 years. 
	- Additional days or years may be granted if there are delays during the passing of the patent.
	- For example, vaccines. 
2. Copyright
	- This is used to grant exclusive rights to the creator of the way in which he expressed an idea. 
	- Copyright usually lasts the lifetime of the creator and fifty years after the creator has passed away.
	- For example, songs.
3. Protection of trade secrets
	- Trade secrets that intellectual property that provides a company or organisation with a competitive edge
	- For example, KFC recipe, Coca Cola recipe
4. Trademarks
	- A symbol, text, sound, smell, taste that uniquely identifies a brand.
	- Nike's tick, Adidas's stripes
	- Some trademarks make their way into the English language, but the trademarks still apply. Companies or organisations that violate the trademarks are still liable. For example, a company must capitalise the p in  photoshop if the word is used commercially.
