## Question 1

### Question A

1. Affects Malaysian's export sector
    - US and China are both Malaysia's top export destination. 
    - The trade war has led to the prices of goods and the cost of manufacturing goods to be more expensive in both countries.
    - This causes the manufacturers to cut production and receive less imports from Malaysia. 
    - In turn, this will cause those working in the export sector to earn less, leading to production cuts and layoffs.
    - Overall, it led to an increased rate of unemployed and the lowered the amount of money in circulation in the economy.
2. Increase in the number of investors in specific sectors
    - Not every sector was in danger due to the trade war. 
    - Semiconductor has, in contrast, seen positive investments from foreign companies.
    - These companies are looking for safer and cheaper alternatives to China and Taiwan.
    - For example, Micron Technology is looking towards South East Asia to reduce their dependence on China and Taiwan.
    - Jensen Huang, the CEO of NVIDIA, a global player in the production of GPUs and AI accelerators, has had multiple talks with the Malaysian prime minister about investments in Malaysia.
3. Exploitation of Malaysia as trans-shipment hub
    - Due to the trade war, China has been seeking ways to avoid the heavy tariffs.
    - One of those ways is to use Malaysia as a trans-shipment hub, as Malaysia was not hit with tariffs as hard as they were. 
    - This presents a heavy risk onto Malaysia as it exposes the country to secondary tariffs and heavy scrutiny from the US.
    - Fortunately, no heavy secondary tariffs have been made yet; in contrast, collaborations have been made between Malaysia and the US to curb these practices.
4. Currency and Financial Market Volatility
    - In the early stages of this trade war, Malaysia's currency had seen a dip in strength as investors pull out due to risk aversion.
    - Although, this made our exports even more attractive, it also increased the cost of living amongst.
5. National Semiconductor Strategy
    - To take advantage of the increased in the number of foreign investors into our semiconductor manufacturing sector, the government has initiated the National Semiconductor Strategy. 
    - This plan aims to cement Malaysia as one of the major players in semiconductor.
    - Historically, Malaysia had only focused on the back-end of semiconductor manufacturing - assembling, testing, and packing.
    - This plan aims to train Malaysian experts to work on the front-end - designing - by training 60,000 experts by 2030.
