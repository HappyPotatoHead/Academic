### Question A ⭐

Monetary and Fiscal policies. 

**Government**
1. Fiscal policy
	- A set of financial strategies that is used by the government
	- This policy revolves around government spending and taxation
		- Government spending affects the infrastructure and employment of the population
		- Taxation affects the amount of money that households or businesses can save or spend
	- During an economic downturn, the government will:
		1. Increase spending
			- This is meant to stimulate economic growth with public investments
			- With increased spending, the new or improved infrastructures and systems can be made
			- This will lead to an increased rate of employment, which will lead to an increase in the amount of money in circulation in the economy
			- This ultimately promotes economic growth
		2. Reduced in taxation
			- This increases the disposable income that households and businesses can have
			- This provides them with more flexibility when they spend their money, incentivising them to spend money. 

**Bank Negara Malaysia**
1. Monetary Policy
	- This is also a set of financial strategy used by the central bank
	- It is designed to:
		1. Control the the overall supply of money
		2. Control liquidity
		3. Influence interest rate
	- It's goals are:
		1. Create sustainable economic growth
		2. Control inflation or deflation
	- During an economic downturn:
		- Open Market Operations
			- The central bank buys government securities from commercials 
			- This increases the amount of money that the commercial banks have in reserve
			- This gives them more flexibility in loaning out money
		- Lowering of loan interest rates
			- This incentivises individuals and businesses to loan money from the bank to expand or start their businesses
			- This is also influenced by the lowering of Overnight Policy Rate, the benchmark for overnight lending between banks
		- Lowering of Required Reserved Rate (RRR) %
			- RRR is the minimum deposit percentage that each commercial bank must have in reserve and cannot be loaned out
			- By lowering the RRR, it allows the banks to loan out more money

## Question 1

### Question A

**Advantages**

1. **J**obs in the export sector will pay more
	- As the global trade barrier falls, countries are more open to trading with each other.
	- This will inevitably lead to increased profits in the export sectors in countries like Malaysia.
	- This allows the companies, agencies or government to be able to pay their workers more
2. **A**llows domestic scene to be exposed to foreign investment, keeping interest rates low
	- With globalisation, foreign direct investments became more feasible and easier
	- As foreigners invest into a country to buy bonds or build companies, the total loanable funds will increase.
	- This ensures that there is sufficient fund for everyone, ensuring interest rates stay low
3. **P**romote global competitiveness
	- With globalisation, not only trades are affected, but also talent.
	- The population of any given country is now exposed to a larger pool of competitors.
	- This forces everyone to work harder to stand out of the larger market.
4. **I**ncrease in productivity
	- The market of any sector in the global stage is constantly changing.
	- This forces companies, agencies, and the government to be constantly on their toes to ensure that they stay relevant.
	- For example, to remain as an attractive exporter of semiconductors, Malaysia has to remain productive in manufacturing high-quality semiconductors
5. **C**heaper trades
	- With globalisation, relationships are formed.
	- Such relationships are often accompanied with benefits such as tariff exemptions, discounts, or free trade area.
	- For example, Malaysia is a part of the Asean Free Trade Area, allowing the government to freely trade with other ASEAN countries
6. **E**ncourage technological advancement and development
	- The diminishing barriers between countries also allows countries to trade new innovations, technology and information with one another.
	- For example, a country may provide other countries with new medical equipment or information.
	- Such exchange can also be done with universities with the student exchange programmes. 

**Disadvantages**

1. Outsourcing of talents
	- Companies are outsourcing their employees from other cheaper countries.
	- This lets them maintain their productivity and output while having lower labour costs.
	- This is especially true in the age of digitalisation where working from home is plausible and has become a common practice. 
		- This affects the majority of white-collar jobs
2. Increased competition
	- Locals are now competing with foreign talents who are equally or even more talented.
	- This makes it even more difficult for locals to stand out.
	- This pushes locals to work even harder and often leads to dissatisfaction as they feel like they are not being prioritised despite being the citizens of a country.  
3. Brain drain
	- Globalisation also exposes locals to other countries where better opportunity may exists.
	- Malaysia has one of the most alarming case of brain drain where approximately 6% of the Malaysian population has left the country with Singapore being the top destination. 
4. Increased vulnerability to global crises
	- Due to the interconnected nature of modern-day global trading, any changes in trading policies between giants of the world may influence the economies of smaller countries.
	- For example, despite not having any direct, immediate consequence on Malaysia, the U.S-China trade war still influenced the Malaysian economy.
		- This is because U.S and China are both Malaysia's top trading partners
5. Exploitation of labour
	- As companies look for labour in countries with lower minimum wages and less stringent rules, it also allows them to be more negligent on the working conditions of these workers.
	- They often have poor working conditions with minimal wages while having to output the same results.

### Question B

1. Competition with known International Brands
	- Despite being the backbone of the economy, SMEs are still under low-value added category
		- This is because customers often default to known international brand
		- This makes it more difficult for SMEs to earn the profit necessary to keep their business going and improve themselves
	- This limits SMEs adoption of newer technology and systems, causing them to lag behind other already established brands.
2. Lack of innovation
	- Malaysia's Percentage of GNP towards R&D is low
	- This means that companies in Malaysia re more focused on using existing technologies than inventing new designs
	- Additionally, Malaysia is also a very OEM-centric nation
		- This means that the country manufactures products from designs that are sent from companies from other nations. 
		- This is generally expected as the nation is an export centric nation, but it ties to the fact that Malaysia does not invest a lot in R&D
	- This also affects SMEs as this means that SMEs who wish to focus in R&D have lower profit overall since the general focus is placed on earning profits with the quick and conventional way 
3. Talent shortage
	- Malaysia is suffering from a severe brain drain
		- Approximately 6% of the Malaysian population has left the nation for opportunities overseas
	- This is bad for SMEs as the number of talented youths are diminishing, making it harder for SMEs to grow as a company. 
	- The mass migration of talents to overseas also makes it difficult for SMEs to compete with MNC as MNC are accepting these talents, accelerating their growths even more. 
4. Lack of the necessary education and skill
	- Although the number of citizens who have access to secondary and tertiary education has increased over the years, the knowledge that these students learn in universities do not translate well to SMEs ready skills.
	- A lot of employers, from both non-SMEs and SMEs have complained that students lack the social skills required. 
	- This discrepancies between what is known by the graduates and what is needed makes hiring difficult for SMEs
