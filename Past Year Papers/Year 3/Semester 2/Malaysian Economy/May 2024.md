**Government**

The government uses fiscal policy. It is a set of financial strategies revolving around government spending and taxation. Government spending affects the infrastructure of the country and subsequently the employment rate of the nation while taxation influences the amount of money that households and businesses can spend or save. 

During economic downturn, the government undergoes expansionary fiscal policy where:
1. Increased government spending
    - This stimulates economic growth with public investments
    - By investing in creating or improving systems or infrastructure, more jobs can be created, leading to an increase in employment rate
    - This will then lead to an increase in money in circulation as more people have the ability to spend their money
2. Reduction in taxation
    - Lowering households taxation allows households and businesses to have more disposable income to save or spend. 
    - This can incentivise them to spend more money, leading to an overall increase in the volume of money in circulation, leading to an economic growth. 

**Bank Negara Malaysia** 

The central bank will enact monetary policy, which is also a set of financial strategies. The monetary policy will change the overall supply of money in the economy, influence exchange rate, and affect liquidity. It's goal is to create a sustainable economic growth and handle deflation and inflation.

During an economic downturn, Bank Negara Malaysia will enact the expansionary monetary policy where:
1. Open Market Operation
    - The central bank will purchase government securities from commercial bank.
    - This helps increase the amount of money these commercial banks have in reserve, effectively increasing their liquidity.
    - This gives them more flexibility for them to loan money to the population and businesses.
2. Lowering of loan interest rate
    - This can incentivise the population or businesses to loan money from the bank to start or expand their own ventures.
    - When this happens, the supply of money in circulation in the economy will increase, stimulating economic growth.
    - The loan interest rate is also affected by the lowering of Overnight Policy Rate, the benchmark for overnight lending between banks.
3. Lowering of Required Reserve Rate
    - This is the minimum percentage of deposit each commercial banks must have that cannot be loaned out.
    - By lowering the RRR, it provides commercial banks with more money to be loan to the public.

These actions are made to improve liquidity in banks, encourage business ventures and spending, leading to economic growth.

**Advantages**

1. **J**obs in the export sector will pay more
    - Globalisation diminishes the global trade barrier.
    - This opens up the market for export in every country.
    - When this happens, the profit from exports for a country like Malaysia increases.
    - This allows those who work in export to have an increase in their wages.
2. **A**llows the domestic scene to have access to foreign investments, keeping interest rate low
    - Globalisation makes it easier for Foreign Direct investments.
    - As foreigners invest in this country to buy bonds, build factories, or companies, the total "loanable fund" increase, ensuring that there is enough money for everyone.
    - This helps keep interest rates low.
3. **P**romote global competition
    - Globalisation exposes the population of any given nation to a wide pool of competition.
    - It is now easier and more probable for a company to fire foreign talents over local talents.
    - This forces locals to work harder to be able to stand out of the global pool of talents.
4. **I**ncrease in productivity
    - The global trade market is volatile and is constantly changing. 
    - This means that every country has to be quick of responds to any trade policy changes, even if those changes do not have direct, immediate consequences.
    - For example, although the U.S. China trade war does not directly affect Malaysia, it will still affect the Malaysian economy as those two nations are Malaysia's top export destinations.
    - This is also because export is one of Malaysia's top GDP contributor.
5. **C**heaper imports
    - With globalisation, relationships will form.
    - These relationships are often accompanied with discounted tariffs, tariff exemption, or free trade area.
    - For example, being a part of ASEAN, Malaysia can enjoy the ASEAN Free Trade Area and trade with other ASEAN countries without heavy tariffs.
6. **E**ncourage technological development and advancements
    - Globalisation allows countries to exchange information, technology, or blueprints to advancements.
    - For example, a country may share their more advanced medical equipment to other country. 
    - The exchange of information is also possible with university programmes such as student exchange programmes.

**Disadvantages**

1. Outsourcing of talents
    - Companies, especially MNCs, are more likely to outsource their talents from other cheaper countries.
    - This, in turn, will reduce the number of roles available to the locals of the country that the company occupies.
    - This forces talents of the country to work harder, often overworking themselves, in order to stand out or sometimes over-qualify for a role.
2. Increased competition
    - As the barrier between nations fall, companies are also given a larger pool of talents to choose from. 
    - If not handled properly, locals may feel dissatisfied as they feel that they are being overlooked due to the perceived notion that overseas talents are inherently better.
    - Students in universities around the world are also now competing with foreign talents.
3. Brain drain
    - As the integration of information and the mobility of the people to cross barriers becomes easier, individuals are exposed to opportunities around the world.
    - Those individuals may be given better opportunities overseas, incentivising them to leave their home-country.
    - This is bad for the development of the nation as talents are leaving the country; they contribute to the development of a foreign country, but not their own.
    - For instance, in Malaysia, approximately 6% of the Malaysian population have left the nation in pursuit of better opportunities overseas.
4. Exploitation of labour
    - Companies, especially MNCs, are hiring individuals from countries that have lower minimum wages and less stringent rules.
    - This allows them to gain similar or even greater profits will spending less on labour fees. 
    - Because of the international aspect of such hiring, prosecuting the companies for poor practices is difficult, leading to poor working environment and unresolved abuse. 
5. Vulnerability to policy changes
    - This is especially true when it involves the trade market between countries.
    - For example, the U.S China trade war has no direct, immediate effect on the Malaysian economy, but it does influence the economy as the two nations are Malaysia's top export destination. 
    - This is also attributed to the fact that export is Malaysia's top GDP contributor.
