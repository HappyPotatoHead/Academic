### Question B
Parallel installation strategy is where the organisation keeps the older system along with the newer system. Once the new system is stable and functional with minimal errors, the management will turn off the older system. The output of the newer system is compared with the older system to determine if the newer system is running as intended or run just as well as the older system. However, since two systems are running simultaneously, it can be very expensive.
### Question C
 Library management system. Deploying the system at a single location lets me identify any existing bugs or performance issues in a controlled environment. This can prevent disruption to the entire system and protect valuable data. By employing the system at a single location, I can train the staff at the pilot site on  how to use the new system. This allows me to further refine the training materials and the aspects that require more training before full rollout. I can also gather feedback from staffs at the pilot location on the functionality and user-friendliness. If anything goes awry, I can contain the damages done onto a single site.

Perfective maintenance is the changes made to the system by adding new features or functionality to the system. Perfective maintenance aims to make the system more complete. Perfective maintenance usually has lower priority compared to corrective maintenance. #### Question II
Separate approach. It costs more as the organisation has to hire and pay another group of programmers to be part of the maintenance team. The organisation also has to provide training to the maintenance team on the system before deploying them, costing more money. In terms of time effectiveness, the maintenance team has to first learn how about the ins and outs of the system, causing less time to be spent on maintaining the system. Communication issues may exists between the programmers and maintenance team, causing delays in work or implementations.
