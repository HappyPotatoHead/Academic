# Case 1: Love Scam

## Legal Aspect

*Love scams or basically any form of scams are punishable under Section 420 of the Penal Code. Section 420 of the Penal Code talks about cheating and dishonesty inducing the delivery of property. *

Love scams in Malaysia are punishable under <mark class="hltr-yellow">Section 420 of the Penal Code</mark>, which revolves around cheating and dishonesty inducing delivery of property. In this case, the scammer will be prosecuted for cheating by personation as he impersonated as an American businessman working in Singapore, tricking the victim into believing that he is legitimate and made her transfer money to him. 
Even if scammer's actual whereabouts are not known, <mark class="hltr-yellow">section 9 of the Computer Crimes Act</mark> can still be applied, since the victim is in Malaysia and Facebook is capable of operation within Malaysia, treating the offence as if it occurred in Malaysia. 

## Ethical

The man has violated the <mark class="hltr-yellow">principle of integrity and honesty</mark>. By impersonating as an American businessman, he masked his true intentions behind a facade, taking advantage of the victim's trust for his own financial gain. The culprit has eroded the trust that the victim has placed on the Internet, potentially causing her to be on alert all the time or just stop using the Internet. Moreover, the culprit has continued the scam for years, completely disregarding the victim's well-being, showing a clear lack of integrity. 

Additionally, the scammer has <mark class="hltr-yellow">taken advantage of the naivety of the victim, showing no respect</mark> and only viewing the victim as a means to an end; the victim is 67 year-old, who may not be experienced in using the Internet or aware of the dangers of the Internet.

# Case 4: Australia's Social Media Age Limit

*Talk about what the law does instead of trying to legalise it*

By passing this law, the government has legally established a new age restriction and usage mandates for social media platforms concerning minors. Specifically, it imposes a legal obligation on social companies to implement age verification mechanisms to further enforce this law. However, the establishment of this law could impact the digital rights of minors, by restricting their independent access and usage time, placing parental consent as a prerequisite for extended usage. This legal framework aims to create a safer environment for minors on social media by introducing legal controls and responsibilities on both parents and social media companies. 

## Ethical Aspect

1. The passing of this law coincide with the <mark class="hltr-yellow">ethical principle of beneficence</mark>. # Case 6: Red Flags to Watch out for in Scams

## Legal

Scams in Malaysia can be investigation under Section 415 to Section 420 of the Penal Code. All of these sections cover cheating of properties and the different ways that the cheating can be done, ranging from impersonation to dishonesty and the punishments for each of these cheating. However, there is no specific cyber laws targeting scams that take place online, instead Malaysia uses a combination of laws such as the Penal Code, Computer Crimes Act, Communications and Multimedia Act, and Personal Data Protection Act. The culprit can be liable to a fine up to fifty thousand ringgit or to be sentenced for up to a year or both and shall also be liable to a further fine of one thousand ringgit for every day during which the offence is continued after conviction.

## Ethical 

In any form of scams or cheating, the ethical principle of <mark class="hltr-yellow">honesty</mark> is violated. This is because for a scam to take place, the culprit has to mask his or her true intention behind a façade to convince a victim to funnel their money to the culprit. For example, in one of the situation provided, the culprit pretends to be a police officer to convince Pak Ahmad into providing his bank account information. 

Additionally, the act of scamming another individual also violates the <mark class="hltr-yellow">respect</mark> ethical principle, as the culprit is only viewing the victim as a means to an end for a financial rather than as an individual with his or her sense of worth, and dignity. To illustrate, the culprit will target vulnerable or individuals who are not aware of scams and create an urgent environment. Taking advantage of the victim's panic, the culprit coerce the victim to provide their personal information. 

However, the article itself coincides with the ethical principle of <mark class="hltr-yellow">benevolence and non-maleficence</mark>. The article is written with the thought of protecting individuals, especially those who are vulnerable - less aware of scams - from being scammed by ill-mannered individuals. By highlighting the prominent red flags to look out for, individuals can avoid falling for these major pitfalls
