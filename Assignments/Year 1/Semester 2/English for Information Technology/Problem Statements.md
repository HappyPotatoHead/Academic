The unbridled usage of social media has affected the dynamic and quality of a romantic relationship especially amongst young adults. Romantic relationships may be affected by the use of social networking services and is associated with a decrease in relationship satisfaction (Marshal et al., 2012; McDaniel & Coyne, 2016, as cited in Abbasi, 2019). Furthermore, the accessibility to interact and communication with anyone online may be the reason of said association. (Douin et al., 2014; Valenzuela et al., 2014, as cited in Abbasi, 2019). Furthermore, consuming content that portrays relationships through rose coloured lenses may distort the users' idea of an ideal relationship (Reizer & Hestroni, 2014, as cited in Abbasi, 2019), bringing them disappointment in their own relationship (Segrin & Nabi,  2002, as cited in Abbasi, 2019). Originally designed for television, the cultivation theory may provide an insight as to how media impacts romantic relationships (Morgan & Shanahan, 2010, as cited in Abbasi, 2019). This theory can be observed when individuals view picture-perfect portrayal of relationships, leading them to accept or internalise irrational standards, beliefs, and evaluations of their less-than-ideal relationship reality. The exploitation of technology has enabled users to fabricate their identity to attract more potential partners and develop relationships that they otherwise would not have initiated (Ellison, Hancock, & Toma, 2011, as cited in Mosley et al., 2020). A detrimental and unpleasant experience for vulnerable users of online dating platforms is thought to be linked with online dating deception, which also threatens the mental health of the victims (Lauckner et al., 2019).
