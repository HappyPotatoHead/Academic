# GDP and Trade

Balance of trade is one of the key factors of any country's GDP. GDP increases when there is a trade surplus, which is what is happening in Malaysia right now. Malaysia has consistently had a trade surplus according to statistics published by DOSM. [^3][^4]

According to DOSM,[^5]

"Malaysia's total trade in **services** increased by **14.6 per cent to record RM497.4 billion in 2024 (2023: RM434.0 billion)**, contributing **25.8 per cent to the Gross Domestic Product (GDP)** at current prices."

Additionally, according to the World Bank, **export** is the primary driver of the Malaysian economy with 71.35% contribution from exports in 2024. [^6][^7]

Having a trade surplus will contribute to a stronger GDP and foreign reserves. However, there are some side effects (_if not handled properly_).

1. Trade surplus[^7]
   - Pros
     - Generally selling more than buying is a good thing
     - Stronger currency
     - More jobs
2. Trade deficit[^8]
   - Pros
     - People get the products they want that they can't get in their own home country
       - Computer chips
     - Foreign investments
     - Weaker currency
   - Cons
     - Poor investment decisions
     - Taxes on imports may be levied

Malaysia's trade performance is also becoming increasingly well.
