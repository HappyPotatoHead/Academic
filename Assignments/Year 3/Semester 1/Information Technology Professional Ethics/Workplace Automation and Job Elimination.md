# Introduction

The exponential advancement of information and communication technologies in the post-pandemic era has outpaced legal developments, particularly with the emergence of artificial intelligence, raising significant ethical and regulatory challenges. If left unaddressed, this regulatory vacuum will inevitably exacerbate cybercrimes, erode public trust in technologies, and foster an exploitative digital environment. As such, it is important for influential organisations and committees to aid in filling the legal vacuum by establishing the necessary code of ethics for both professional and non-professional settings, curbing unbridled illicit activities while the laws are still being formulated. For instance, the Malaysia Board of Technologists, MBOT, has formulated a code of ethics that is widely implemented across technology-related sectors in Malaysia, including education, research, and industry[^1]. 

These challenges underscore the need for such ethical frameworks, as illustrated by the growing apprehension amongst the working class regarding workplace automation and job displacement while misinformation on social media remains rampant despite significant efforts to address it. Both concerns have been intensified by the explosive breakthroughs in recent years, specifically large language models, which have simplified automation and content generation while making it easily accessible. This report will investigate the ethical imperative within the technology industry, focusing on both the ubiquity and impact of artificial intelligence on employment, as well as the spread of pervasive and manipulative misinformation on social media. 

# Workplace automation and Job elimination

## Explain the ethical concern and its relevance to IT

The forefront of ethical concerns in regard to workplace automation and job elimination is the job displacement itself. Mass job displacement can lead to economic hardship for individuals, especially those who lack the opportunity to pursue an education, given the growing value of a degree today. Moreover, companies across the globe have undergone mass layoffs, which if not handled properly, could cause societal instability. High unemployment has been proven to cause catastrophic effects on individuals such as worsening physical and mental health or the erosion of familial relationships[^2]. Work is often more than just a paycheck; it is tied to the individual's identity and a sense of purpose, leading to depression and other psychological problems. [^3] From a utilitarian standpoint, it is imperative for companies to weigh efficiency and productivity against the social and economic effects inflicted upon displaced workers. 

This ethical concern is profoundly relevant to the IT sector; they are actively designing, developing, and maintaining systems that automate human tasks. These systems are becoming increasingly capable, evolving from performing small yet frequent tasks such as data entry or customer service to complex tasks such as data analysis or some supervisory role, albeit imperfect. The speed at which such a system is evolving, with various theories and prospects about AGI, creates an unprecedented speed in job transformation, leading to workers caught off guard by job displacements. This further solidifies the need to impose a direct ethical responsibility on the developers to consider the human consequences of implementing such powerful tools into the global workforce. 

## Analyse real-life incidents or case studies

The major motive behind the extensive integration of AI in the workflow of any company is the improved productivity and efficiency while saving costs. For instance, on the 17th of June 2025, Jassy, CEO of Amazon, disseminated a memo to his employees that highlighted how AI has transformed operations, leading to anticipated cuts in Amazon's corporate workforce.[^4] This signifies a broader trend where companies increasingly implement AI to handle tasks once handled by entry-level or repetitive roles, such as data processing and basic customer inquiries. As Julian Fayed noted, a lot of their lower-level operations are managed by AI 24/7.[^5] This newfound efficiency and productivity reduce the need to hire and invest in fresh graduates, thereby intensifying competition in an already tight job market. 

However, seasoned employees are not invulnerable to AI's advancements; in May 2025, CrowdStrike announced a 5% workforce reduction, with CEO George Kurtz explicitly stating that "AI flattens our hiring curve, and helps us innovate from idea to product faster"[^6]. This reaffirms the use of AI in replacing established roles by automating increasingly complex tasks that previously required human cognitive effort. 

The automation of tasks and layoffs have led to an increase in the unemployment rate around the world. A study titled "Assessing AI-induced Job-displacement in Malaysia" projects that approximately 1, 575, 970 workers would be displaced across various occupational categories by 2026 due to AI adoption. [^7] For such a large-scale shift towards AI, it is imperative to establish ethical frameworks across the globe to ensure employees' welfare is kept.  

# Conclusion

In essence, this report has examined the accelerated development of AI and the unbridled spread of misinformation on social media platforms. These issues demand the need for the establishment of ethical frameworks across professional domains. Due to the widespread adoption of AI, workplace automation has been escalating, leading to apprehension surrounding job security and displacement, resulting in the need for proactive measures to safeguard societal well-being. If left unmitigated, this trend risks an increase in life dissatisfaction and erosion of the quality of life.

Furthermore, while the widespread accessibility of the Internet is to be celebrated, it has simultaneously rendered efforts to combat misinformation challenging, thereby solidifying the need for ethical conduct amongst netizens, governments, and social media platforms alike, lest digital chaos ensues. 

To mitigate these escalating challenges, it is crucial for influential bodies, such as the MBOT, to continue their leadership in formulating robust codes of ethics. These codes of ethics serve as guideposts, fostering accountability while legal frameworks catch up with ongoing advancements. Ultimately, preventing exploitation and digital chaos hinges on our conscious effort and collective commitment to prioritise human well-being in this rapidly evolving digital landscape. 

# Rejected 
Rather than attempting to develop an AI system to replace all jobs, individuals developing such systems ought to comprehend which roles are safe to be completely replaced with AI and which roles would be elevated having AI as an assistant instead. 

Another major concern is the use of biased, harmful, or misleading data to train AI algorithms. An AI mimics the data in which it was trained on. If the training data reflects human prejudice, historical discrimination, or contains imbalances, the algorithm will learn and perpetuate these biases. In 2018, MIT and Stanford University researchers found that the error rates of artificial intelligence programs determining the gender of light-skinned men were below 0.8% while the error error rates rose up to 34% for darker-skinned women. This is incredibly alarming as companies are now experimenting with AI in hiring.

Such biases originated in the development lifecycle. Data scientists and AI engineers make crucial decisions about data collection, pre-processing, feature engineering, algorithm design, and model training. Their inherent biases, lack of diverse perspectives, or lack of a meticulous process can inadvertently embed bias into the system.
